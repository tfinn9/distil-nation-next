There's no single "right" way in, but a few habits make the learning curve much shorter and more enjoyable.

## 1. Taste across categories before you specialise

Rather than buying five whiskies in a row, try one whisky, one gin, one rum and one vodka early on. Understanding how different the *categories* taste from each other makes it much easier to notice smaller differences within a category later.

## 2. Taste side by side, not one at a time

It's hard to remember what a spirit tasted like a week ago. Pouring two small measures next to each other — even a well-known bottle against something new — makes differences in aroma, sweetness, texture and finish far more obvious.

## 3. Taste neutral first

Try spirits neat (or with a small amount of water) before mixing them into cocktails, at least the first time. Mixers can mask a lot of character, and getting a baseline read on a spirit helps you understand what it will and won't do in a drink.

## 4. Use your nose as much as your mouth

Most of what we perceive as "taste" in spirits is actually smell. Swirl the glass, let it breathe for a minute, and nose it before sipping — you'll pick up far more.

## 5. Keep loose notes

You don't need formal tasting sheets. Even a one-line note ("sweeter than I expected, vanilla, short finish") after each new bottle builds a personal reference library fast.

## 6. Let curiosity lead, not price

Some of the most interesting spirits to try when starting out are inexpensive, widely available bottles that clearly represent their style — a straightforward London Dry gin, an approachable single malt, a classic golden rum. You don't need to spend big to learn the fundamentals, and New Zealand's growing craft scene means there are plenty of accessibly priced local options to start with.
