Gin is built in two stages: a base spirit, then a flavouring process.

## 1. The base spirit

Most gin starts life as a **neutral grain spirit** — a highly rectified, near-flavourless alcohol usually distilled from wheat, barley or corn. Some producers use grape or molasses-based neutral spirit instead. At this stage it's not meaningfully different from a base vodka.

## 2. The botanicals

What turns that neutral spirit into gin is redistillation or infusion with **botanicals** — and by legal definition, juniper berries must be the dominant flavour. Beyond juniper, distillers typically add a mix of:

- Citrus peel (orange, lemon)
- Coriander seed
- Angelica root
- Orris root
- Cardamom, cinnamon or other spices
- Local or native botanicals — this is where New Zealand distillers get creative, using things like kawakawa, horopito, mānuka or native citrus

## How the botanicals are added

There are two common methods:

- **Redistillation (vapour infusion or one-shot distillation)** — botanicals are added to the still along with the neutral spirit and redistilled together, so their oils and aromatics carry over into the final spirit.
- **Cold compounding / infusion** — botanicals are steeped in the neutral spirit without a second distillation. This is cheaper and faster but generally considered a lower-quality method for "gin" versus a proper redistilled product.
