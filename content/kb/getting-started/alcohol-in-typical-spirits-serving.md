A typical bar "nip" or pour of spirits in New Zealand is **30ml**. At the common bottling strength of 40% ABV, that pour contains:

```
30ml × 40% = 12ml of pure alcohol
```

Using the standard-drink formula from our companion article ("What is a standard drink in New Zealand?"), that works out to roughly **1.2 standard drinks** per 30ml pour at 40% ABV.

## How this changes with strength

The stronger the spirit, the more alcohol is packed into the same pour size:

- **37.5% ABV, 30ml** → ≈1.1 standard drinks
- **40% ABV, 30ml** → ≈1.2 standard drinks
- **46% ABV, 30ml** → ≈1.4 standard drinks
- **58% ABV cask strength, 30ml** → ≈1.7 standard drinks

## Why this matters when tasting flights or cocktails

A full-strength cocktail often contains 45–60ml of spirit once you add multiple ingredients, which can easily add up to two or more standard drinks in a single glass — even though it "feels" like one drink because it's mixed. Being aware of pour size and ABV together (rather than either alone) gives a much more accurate picture of how much alcohol you're actually consuming.
