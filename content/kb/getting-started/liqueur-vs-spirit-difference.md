The simplest way to think about it: **every liqueur starts with a spirit, but not every spirit is a liqueur.**

## Spirits

A spirit (whisky, gin, vodka, rum, brandy) is a distilled alcoholic drink, generally unsweetened, bottled at a relatively high strength — typically 37.5–50% ABV or higher.

## Liqueurs

A liqueur takes a spirit as its base and adds:

- **Sugar or other sweetener** — liqueurs are, by definition, sweetened
- **Flavouring** — fruit, herbs, spices, coffee, cream, nuts, or botanicals
- Usually **lower ABV** than the base spirit, often somewhere between 15% and 30% ABV, though some liqueurs are bottled much stronger

Examples include coffee liqueurs, orange liqueurs (like triple sec or Cointreau-style products), herbal liqueurs, and cream liqueurs. New Zealand distillers have leaned into liqueurs using local ingredients — kawakawa, mānuka honey, native berries and coffee are common flavour directions.

## Why it matters in a bottle shop

Liqueurs are built to be sipped, mixed into cocktails, or poured over dessert — their sweetness makes them a very different drinking experience from a spirit meant to be tasted neat or built into a stirred cocktail. If a label says "liqueur," expect noticeably more sweetness and a lower proof than the equivalent spirit category.
