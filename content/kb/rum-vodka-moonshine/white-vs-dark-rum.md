The core difference between white and dark rum comes down to one variable: **time in wood**.

## White rum

White (or "light" / "silver") rum is typically bottled shortly after distillation, either completely unaged or aged briefly before being filtered (often through charcoal) to strip out any colour it picked up. The result is a clean, relatively neutral spirit with subtle sugar-cane character, which is why it's the standard base for cocktails like a Mojito or Daiquiri — it adds body and a light sweetness without competing with other ingredients.

## Dark rum

Dark rum is aged in oak barrels for a meaningfully longer period, picking up colour and flavour from the wood the same way whisky does — vanilla, caramel, dried fruit, spice, and a deeper, richer body. Some darker colour can also come from added caramel colouring in certain products, which is worth knowing when judging a rum by colour alone — colour isn't a reliable proxy for actual age.

## Gold/amber rum — the middle ground

Between the two sits gold or amber rum, aged for a shorter period than a "dark" or "aged" rum, giving some colour and mellowing without the full depth of a longer-aged product.

## Which to choose

White rum is generally the better mixing spirit for classic cocktails that want a clean sugar-cane backbone. Dark and aged rum is better suited to sipping neat or in cocktails (like a dark 'n' stormy or an aged rum Old Fashioned) where you want the wood-derived character to come through.
