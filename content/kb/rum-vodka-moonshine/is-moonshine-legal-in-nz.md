There's an important distinction between the **product** (an unaged, "moonshine-style" spirit) and **how it was made**.

## Buying and selling moonshine: legal, if licensed

A New Zealand distillery can legally produce and sell an unaged, "moonshine-style" spirit exactly like it would any other spirit, provided it holds the required excise manufacturer's licence and complies with the same labelling, tax and sale rules as any other spirit producer. Several New Zealand craft distilleries release white/unaged spirits under a "moonshine" or "white dog" style name.

## Making your own at home: generally not legal

The historical, illicit sense of "moonshine" — distilling spirits yourself without a licence, whether or not you sell it — runs into New Zealand's excise laws. Under the Customs and Excise Act 2018, distilling spirits requires a licence, and this applies even to spirit made for personal use, which is a stricter position than New Zealand's rules around home-brewing beer or making your own wine (which are generally permitted for personal, non-commercial use).

## Why spirits are treated differently from beer and wine

Excise on alcohol is calculated per litre of pure alcohol, and spirits carry a meaningfully higher rate than beer or wine of equivalent volume — which is part of why home distillation, unlike home-brewing, is tightly controlled: unlicensed spirit production represents a bigger potential loss of excise revenue and a bigger safety concern (poorly made spirit can be genuinely dangerous, including risks like methanol contamination).
