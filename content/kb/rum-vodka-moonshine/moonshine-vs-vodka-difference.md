At a glance, moonshine and vodka can look identical — both are usually clear, both are often high-proof — but they're built with opposite goals in mind.

## The production philosophy is different

- **Moonshine** is essentially unaged whisky — made using a whisky-style production process (grain mash, fermentation, pot or column distillation) but bottled before it ever sees a barrel. The distiller isn't trying to strip out grain character; the point is often to showcase it.
- **Vodka** is deliberately distilled multiple times (often through column stills) and frequently filtered afterward, specifically to remove as much flavour and raw-ingredient character as possible, leaving a clean, neutral spirit.

## Flavour comparison

Moonshine typically has a noticeable, sometimes assertive grain sweetness and can carry more heat and rougher edges, depending on how it was cut and distilled. Vodka aims for a much cleaner, smoother, more neutral profile with little raw-ingredient character coming through.

## Legal category differences

In many jurisdictions, "vodka" is a defined category with rectification/purity requirements, while "moonshine" is more of a style or historical term than a strict legal category — a producer might label a spirit "moonshine" largely because of its unaged, whisky-adjacent story rather than any fixed production standard.

## Base ingredients can overlap

Both spirits can be made from grain, so the ingredient list alone won't necessarily tell you which is which — the deciding factors are the distillation approach (how many times, and to what purity) and whether the goal was neutrality (vodka) or raw character (moonshine).
