Unaged whisky — often called "new make spirit" or "white dog" — is essentially whisky before it's spent any time in a barrel. It's a useful way to understand exactly what maturation actually adds, because everything you taste beyond this base has come from the wood.

## What you'll actually taste

- **Raw grain character** — a noticeable sweetness and cereal note from whatever grain was used (corn tends toward a rounder sweetness, rye toward spice, malted barley toward a biscuity, slightly nutty note)
- **A sharper, hotter alcohol presence** — without the mellowing effect of time in wood and evaporation, unaged spirit often feels more aggressive on the palate at the same ABV
- **Sometimes a slightly oily or vegetal note**, especially from fusel compounds retained in the cut chosen during distillation (see "What are heads, hearts and tails?")
- **No colour** — one of the clearest visual tells; unaged spirit is completely clear, since colour comes almost entirely from barrel contact

## Why distillers release it at all

Some producers bottle and sell unaged spirit directly (marketed as moonshine, white whiskey or new-make spirit) as a style in its own right, and some release it specifically so drinkers can taste the "before" alongside an aged expression from the same distillery — an educational, side-by-side comparison that makes the effect of barrel ageing very obvious.

## The takeaway

If you want to genuinely understand how much of a whisky's flavour comes from the barrel versus the raw spirit itself, tasting an unaged new-make alongside its aged counterpart is one of the most direct ways to see it.
