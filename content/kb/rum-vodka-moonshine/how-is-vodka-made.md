Vodka's whole production philosophy is built around purity rather than showcasing raw-ingredient character, which shapes every step of the process.

## 1. Choose a base

Grain (wheat, rye, corn) and potatoes are the most traditional bases, though grapes, sugar beet, whey and other fermentable sources are also used — see "What is vodka made from?" for more detail. Because vodka is distilled and filtered so thoroughly, the base matters less to the final flavour than it does for a spirit like whisky or rum.

## 2. Fermentation

The base ingredient is mashed (if starch-based, this includes converting starch to sugar) and fermented with yeast to produce a low-alcohol wash, similar to the early stages of whisky or rum production.

## 3. Distillation

Vodka is typically distilled multiple times, and very commonly through **column stills**, which are especially effective at producing a highly rectified, close-to-pure ethanol output compared to pot stills. Some producers distil three, five, or even more times, marketing this as a mark of purity and smoothness.

## 4. Filtration

After distillation, many vodkas are filtered again — commonly through activated charcoal, though some brands use other media (like quartz sand or even diamond dust, marketed more for novelty than any dramatically provable flavour effect) — to remove any remaining trace compounds and further smooth the spirit.

## 5. Dilution and bottling

The highly rectified spirit is diluted with water down to bottling strength (commonly 37.5–40% ABV, sometimes higher) and bottled — vodka is not aged in wood.
