Tonic water isn't neutral — its quinine bitterness and sugar level interact directly with a gin's botanicals, so matching the two thoughtfully makes a real difference to the final drink.

## Classic/London Dry style gins

A juniper-forward, classic London Dry gin generally pairs well with a **traditional Indian tonic water**, which has enough quinine bitterness and carbonation to stand up to (and complement) that strong juniper backbone without getting lost.

## Citrus-forward gins

Gins built around citrus peel do well with a **light or "Mediterranean-style" tonic**, which tends to be less bitter and sweeter, letting the citrus character shine rather than competing with strong quinine bitterness.

## Floral or delicate contemporary gins

Lighter, more floral or delicately botanical gins can be overwhelmed by a strong, bitter tonic. A **light/low-quinine tonic** or even a high-quality soda water with a delicate garnish often shows these gins off better than a heavy classic tonic would.

## Native/New Zealand botanical gins

Gins built around native ingredients like kawakawa, horopito or mānuka often benefit from a **simple, clean tonic** that doesn't compete with those distinctive flavours — an overly sweet or heavily flavoured tonic can easily drown out subtler native botanical notes.

## The general rule

Start with a good-quality, not-too-sweet tonic as your baseline, and adjust from there: if the gin's character is getting lost, move toward a lighter or less bitter tonic; if the gin feels thin against the tonic, a stronger classic tonic will usually balance it better.
