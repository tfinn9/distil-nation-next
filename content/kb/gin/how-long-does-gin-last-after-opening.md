Gin doesn't spoil the way food does — its high ABV makes it inhospitable to the microbes that cause food to go off. What actually changes over time in an opened bottle is more subtle: gradual flavour drift, not safety.

## What actually happens after opening

Once opened, a bottle has more airspace exposed to oxygen, and very slow oxidation and evaporation can occur over a long period. Volatile aromatic compounds — especially bright citrus and delicate floral botanicals — are the first to fade, which is why an old, mostly-empty bottle of gin can start to taste flatter or duller than a fresh one, even though it's still perfectly safe to drink.

## A practical guideline

As a rough rule of thumb, most gin holds its best flavour for **around 1–2 years** after opening if stored properly (see storage tips below), though it will remain drinkable well beyond that — it simply may not taste quite as vibrant.

## How to store an opened bottle

- Keep it sealed tightly between uses
- Store upright, away from direct sunlight and heat
- A cool, stable cupboard is ideal — the fridge or freezer is also fine if you prefer gin served cold (see "Should gin be stored in the freezer?")

If you have a bottle that's been open for a long time and it's largely finished, it's a good excuse to taste it against a fresh bottle of the same gin — a fun way to notice exactly how much flavour drift has actually happened.
