"Botanical distillery" isn't a strictly regulated or legally defined term — it's generally used to describe a distillery whose core focus is building spirits (usually gin) around a curated selection of plant-based ingredients, rather than, say, a distillery primarily focused on grain whisky or column-distilled vodka.

## What typically sets a botanical distillery apart

- A strong emphasis on **sourcing and selecting botanicals** — sometimes foraged locally, sometimes grown on-site, sometimes sourced from specific regions for a particular character
- Recipes built around **showcasing specific plants** (native, foraged or regionally distinctive) rather than simply following a standard gin template
- Often smaller-batch production, since sourcing and preparing distinctive botanicals doesn't scale the same way a large, standardised operation does

## New Zealand examples of the concept

Several New Zealand gin producers position themselves around this idea — highlighting native ingredients like mānuka, kawakawa, horopito, or coastal and alpine botanicals unique to their specific region, turning the distillery's location itself into part of the product story.

## Why it matters to drinkers

If a brand describes itself as a "botanical distillery," it's usually signalling that botanical selection (not just base-spirit quality or still design) is the heart of their process and their point of difference — worth paying attention to which specific botanicals they highlight, since that's likely to be where their gin's character comes from.
