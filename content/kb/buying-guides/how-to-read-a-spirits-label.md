Once you know what to look for, a spirits label can tell you a surprising amount before you've even opened the bottle.

## ABV / proof

Tells you the strength — see "What does ABV mean?" A higher ABV (especially cask strength whisky) generally signals a more concentrated, less-diluted spirit.

## Age statement

For whisky and other aged spirits, an age statement (e.g. "12 Year Old") reflects the **youngest** whisky in the bottle if it's a blend of multiple ages — older components may be included, but the label reflects the minimum. No age statement doesn't necessarily mean young or lower quality — some producers deliberately release "no age statement" (NAS) products to allow more flexible blending.

## Cask type / finish

Terms like "ex-bourbon," "sherry cask," "port finish" tell you what the spirit matured in, which is one of the biggest flavour signals on the whole label — see our articles on barrel ageing and why different barrels create different flavours.

## Single malt / single cask / cask strength

Each of these terms has a specific meaning (see our dedicated articles) — worth knowing the difference rather than assuming they're interchangeable marketing terms.

## Region / origin

For whisky and gin especially, region can hint at style tendencies (though it's not a guarantee) — and for New Zealand spirits, origin often signals which native botanicals or local water source were used.

## Standard drinks and ingredients

New Zealand labels are required to show standard drink counts (see "What is a standard drink in New Zealand?") and relevant allergen or ingredient information.

## Batch or bottle numbers

Small-batch or limited releases often carry a batch/bottle number — a signal of limited production, though not by itself a guarantee of superior quality.
