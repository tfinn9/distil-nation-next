Gin shopping benefits from thinking about intended use just as much as flavour preference, since the category spans such a wide stylistic range.

## Check the botanical bill

Most gin labels or brand websites list the key botanicals used. If you already know you like a juniper-forward, classic profile, look for language like "London Dry" or "classic." If you're drawn to something more distinctive, look for gins built around a standout botanical — citrus, floral, or a New Zealand native ingredient like mānuka, kawakawa or horopito.

## Match the gin to the drink

- **Gin and tonic** — a robust, juniper-forward gin generally holds up best against tonic's bitterness.
- **Martini** — a cleaner, drier gin with good balance tends to work best, since there's nowhere for flaws to hide in such a simple drink.
- **Gin-based cocktails with strong other flavours** (Negroni, Tom Collins) — a gin with a bit more character can stand up well against other bold ingredients.

## Consider ABV

Most gin sits around 37.5–47% ABV — higher-ABV "navy strength" gins (57%+) pack more intensity and can be a better choice for cocktails that dilute the spirit significantly (like a long drink with lots of tonic or juice).

## Support local where it interests you

New Zealand's gin scene is large and varied (it's the most common spirit category in our own distillery directory) — if you're curious about local craft distilling, gin is generally the easiest and most affordable entry point to start exploring.

## Try small before buying big

Many bars and bottle shops offer smaller-format bottles or tasting flights — a good way to explore a few unfamiliar gins before committing to a full-size bottle.
