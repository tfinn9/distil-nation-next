Whisky buying gets much easier once you separate "what style is this" from "is this a good bottle" — they're different questions.

## Decide how you'll drink it

- **Neat/sipping** — look toward single malts with a flavour profile you already know you enjoy (fruity, sherried, peated, etc.)
- **On ice or with a splash of water** — most whisky styles work here; slightly bolder, higher-ABV whiskies can hold up especially well to dilution.
- **Mixing into cocktails** — a solid blended whisky or a straightforward bourbon is often a better (and more cost-effective) choice than a delicate, expensive single malt whose subtlety would get lost in a mixed drink.

## Consider style preferences you already know

If you've enjoyed a peated whisky before, look for that on the label (see "What is peated whisky?"). If you know you prefer a sweeter, fruitier profile, sherry-cask or ex-bourbon-matured expressions are a reasonable starting signal — though always worth checking reviews or tasting notes rather than relying on cask type alone.

## Don't over-index on age

An older age statement generally costs more, but doesn't automatically mean "better for you" — see "How long does whisky need to age?" for why age alone isn't the full story.

## For New Zealand whisky specifically

Given the local industry's youth, look for producer story and cask sourcing information — many New Zealand distilleries are transparent about their cask programme and maturation approach, which can help set expectations for a still-developing category (see "What is unique about New Zealand whisky?").

## When in doubt, ask for a taste

If you're buying from a bottle shop or bar with an open bottle, ask for a small taste before committing — this beats guessing from the label alone every time.
