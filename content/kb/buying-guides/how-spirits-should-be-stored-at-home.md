Spirits are more stable and forgiving than wine, but a few basic habits keep every bottle tasting its best for longer.

## Store upright

Keep bottles standing up, not lying on their side. Prolonged contact between a cork and high-ABV liquid can degrade the cork over time, affecting the seal — unlike wine, where lying bottles on their side is often recommended to keep the cork moist.

## Avoid direct sunlight and heat

UV light and heat can gradually degrade flavour compounds and, over long periods, affect colour. A cupboard, cabinet, or shelf away from windows and heat sources (stoves, heaters, sunny sills) is ideal for any spirit — whisky, gin, rum or vodka alike.

## Keep the seal tight between pours

Once opened, reseal the cap or cork firmly after each use. More airspace and looser seals speed up the very slow oxidation and evaporation that can dull flavour in an opened bottle over time (see our articles on how long gin and whisky last after opening).

## Room temperature is fine for almost everything

Most spirits don't need refrigeration — gin and vodka are sometimes chilled or frozen by preference for serving (see "Should gin be stored in the freezer?"), but this is a serving choice, not a storage requirement.

## Keep opened bottles moving

Spirits taste their best relatively soon after opening — if you have a large collection, it's worth prioritising drinking through opened bottles rather than letting several sit half-full for years, since flavour (though not safety) can drift over very long periods.
