Whisky pricing spans an enormous range, and the price tag alone tells you surprisingly little about how much you'll personally enjoy a bottle.

## What actually drives price

- **Age** — longer maturation means more time occupying an expensive barrel, more evaporation loss (the "angel's share"), and more warehouse storage cost — all of which get passed on.
- **Cask type and finishing** — rare or premium cask types (first-fill sherry, unusual finishes) cost more to source than standard ex-bourbon casks.
- **Scarcity and limited releases** — small batches and one-off releases command a premium simply because supply is limited, independent of any quality difference.
- **Brand and marketing** — packaging, brand heritage and marketing spend all factor into price, sometimes disproportionately to what's actually in the bottle.

## A rough way to think about tiers

- **Everyday/entry bottles** — solid, reliable whisky for regular drinking, mixing, or simply learning a style.
- **Mid-range** — often the sweet spot for quality-to-price ratio, including many well-regarded single malts.
- **Premium/special occasion** — long-aged, limited or rare releases, where you're paying partly for scarcity and story as much as flavour.

## The real answer

Spend what you're comfortable with, and treat price as one input, not the deciding factor. Plenty of excellent whisky — including from New Zealand's own smaller producers — sits well below the price of famous international brands simply because it hasn't built the same marketing scale, not because it tastes worse.
