**Usually, yes — but not for the reasons many people think.**

Walk into any liquor store and you'll quickly notice the price difference.

A mainstream gin might cost $50. A locally distilled New Zealand craft gin might cost $80 or $90. The same pattern appears with whisky, rum, vodka, and liqueurs.

So, what exactly are you paying for?

The short answer is that you're not just buying alcohol. You're often paying for smaller production runs, local ingredients, more hands-on production, and the creativity of an independent producer.

That doesn't automatically make every craft spirit better, but it does explain why many bottles cost more.

## Small batches cost more to produce

Large international brands produce enormous volumes of spirits.

They buy ingredients in bulk, automate production, negotiate better packaging prices, and spread their overhead costs across millions of bottles.

Craft distilleries don't have those advantages.

A small New Zealand distillery might produce a few hundred bottles from a single batch, while a multinational producer could produce hundreds of thousands.

The economics are completely different.

## Ingredients matter

Many New Zealand distilleries use locally sourced ingredients that simply cost more.

Depending on the spirit, those ingredients might include:

* Native botanicals
* Local honey
* Seasonal fruit
* Specialty grains
* Wine barrels from New Zealand vineyards

Those ingredients often create more distinctive flavours, but they also increase production costs.

## Much more of the process is done by hand

Many craft distilleries still rely heavily on manual production.

That can include:

* Hand-cutting botanicals
* Monitoring individual distillation runs
* Hand-filling bottles
* Applying labels by hand
* Wax-dipping bottles
* Packaging orders manually

When you buy a craft spirit, you're often buying something made through a much more hands-on process.

## You're paying for experimentation

Large brands tend to focus on consistency.

Craft distillers often focus on creativity.

That's why you'll find New Zealand producers experimenting with:

* Native botanicals
* Smoked ingredients
* Unusual barrel finishes
* Seasonal releases
* Small-batch collaborations

Some experiments become award-winning spirits.

Others become one-off releases that never appear again.

That willingness to experiment is part of what makes craft spirits interesting.

## More expensive doesn't automatically mean better

This is probably the most important point.

A $90 bottle isn't automatically better than a $50 bottle.

Taste is subjective.

Some people prefer classic international brands because they're familiar and consistent. Others enjoy discovering new flavours and supporting local producers.

The label doesn't guarantee quality.

The liquid in the bottle does.

## So, how do you decide whether a craft spirit is worth it?

Ask yourself a few simple questions:

* Am I looking for something unique?
* Do I want to support a local producer?
* Am I interested in the story behind the bottle?
* Is flavour my top priority?
* Would I rather have consistency or experimentation?

If you're answering "yes" to any of those questions, a craft spirit may absolutely be worth the extra money.

And if you're still unsure, start with a tasting flight at a local distillery.

It's often the cheapest way to discover whether a particular producer deserves a permanent place on your shelf.
