"Craft" spirits generally cost more than large-scale commercial brands, and there are real cost drivers behind that — but it's worth understanding what you're actually paying for.

## Why craft spirits typically cost more

- **Smaller batch sizes** mean higher per-bottle production cost — a small distillery doesn't get the economies of scale a large producer does on raw materials, packaging, or equipment utilisation.
- **More hands-on labour** — many craft producers are far more manual in their process (hand-bottling, hand-labelling, closely monitored distillation) compared to highly automated large-scale operations.
- **Distinctive or premium ingredients** — local, native or hard-to-source botanicals and raw materials (a common feature of New Zealand craft gin, for example) often cost more than commodity ingredients used at scale.
- **Lower production volume relative to fixed costs** — a small distillery still has to cover the cost of its still, premises and licensing across a much smaller number of bottles sold.

## Where the premium doesn't automatically mean "better"

None of this guarantees a craft spirit will taste better than a well-made large-scale brand — skill, recipe development and quality control still vary distillery to distillery, regardless of size. Some craft spirits are genuinely exceptional; others are still finding their feet as a newer producer.

## A practical way to decide

Rather than assuming "craft = better" or "craft = overpriced," it's worth tasting and judging each bottle on its own merits — and factoring in things that matter to you beyond flavour alone, like supporting local producers, provenance, or trying something genuinely distinctive that a mass-market brand wouldn't offer.
