Most whisky is diluted with water before bottling, brought down from barrel strength (commonly somewhere in the 55–65% ABV range after years of maturation, though it varies) to a standard bottling strength like 40% or 43% ABV.

**Cask strength** (sometimes called "barrel proof" or "full proof") whisky skips that dilution step and is bottled at (or very close to) the strength it reached naturally inside the cask. That means:

- Higher ABV — often 55–65%+, though it varies cask to cask
- More concentrated flavour, since nothing has been diluted
- No two cask-strength releases are identical, because every barrel matures to a slightly different final strength

## Why drinkers seek it out

Cask strength gives you the whisky closest to how it actually tasted coming out of the barrel, before a blender's dilution choices shape the final result. It also gives the drinker control — you can add your own water gradually and find the exact strength and flavour balance you prefer (see "Should you add water to whisky?").

## What to expect when tasting it

Cask strength whisky can taste hotter and more intense neat than a standard-strength bottling, and a small amount of water is often recommended to "open up" the aromatics — but there's no rule requiring it. Many drinkers enjoy at least their first taste completely neat to judge the spirit at full strength.
