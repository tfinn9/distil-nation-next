There's a persistent myth that adding water to whisky is somehow disrespectful or "wrong." In reality, many distillers, blenders and whisky judges add a small amount of water themselves when formally evaluating a spirit.

## Why water can help

Whisky's alcohol content can suppress certain aromatic compounds and numb the nose and palate, especially at cask strength (55%+ ABV). Adding a small amount of water lowers the ABV slightly, which can:

- Reduce the "burn" that masks subtler aromas
- Change how volatile compounds evaporate off the surface of the glass, sometimes revealing new notes
- Soften the texture on the palate

## How much to add

There's no fixed ratio — start small. A few drops at a time, tasting between additions, lets you find the point where the whisky opens up without becoming diluted and flat. Standard-strength whiskies (around 40–43% ABV) often need little or no water; cask-strength whiskies (55%+ ABV) benefit more from a small addition for most drinkers.

## Water quality matters a little

Still, room-temperature water (not sparkling, not straight from the tap if it's heavily chlorinated or mineral-heavy) is the usual recommendation, since strong off-flavours in the water can otherwise interfere with what you're tasting.

## Ultimately, it's your glass

There's no "correct" answer for a given bottle — the goal is to taste the whisky the way you enjoy it most. If you prefer it completely neat, that's a perfectly valid choice too.
