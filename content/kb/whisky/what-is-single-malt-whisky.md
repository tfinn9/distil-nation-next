"Single malt" is one of the most misunderstood terms on a whisky label. It refers to two specific things:

- **Single** — the whisky was distilled at **one distillery** (not that it came from a single barrel or a single batch)
- **Malt** — it was made entirely from **malted barley** (as opposed to other grains like corn, wheat or rye)

## What single malt does NOT mean

- It does not mean single cask — a single malt is usually a blend of many casks from the same distillery, combined to create a consistent house style (unless specifically labelled "single cask" or "single barrel").
- It does not mean a single vintage/year — most single malts blend whisky from multiple years of production, unless a specific vintage is stated.

## Single malt vs blended malt vs blended whisky

- **Single malt** — 100% malted barley, one distillery.
- **Blended malt** — 100% malted barley, but combining whisky from more than one distillery.
- **Blended whisky** — a mix of malt whisky and grain whisky (see "What is blended whisky?"), often from multiple distilleries.

## Why single malt has a premium reputation

Because it comes from one distillery's stills, water source and house style, single malt is often seen as a more distinctive, "signature" expression of a particular producer, compared to a blend built for broad, consistent appeal across many casks and sometimes many distilleries. New Zealand's whisky scene — including producers like Cardrona Distillery — has focused heavily on single malt as its flagship style.
