Non-peated (or "unpeated") whisky is simply whisky where the malted barley was dried without burning peat — typically using hot air or another clean-burning fuel source instead. Without the peat smoke, the malt doesn't pick up the smoky phenolic compounds that define peated whisky (see "What is peated whisky?").

## What it tastes like instead

Without smoke as a flavour driver, non-peated whisky's character comes primarily from the grain itself, the yeast and fermentation, the still shape, and — most significantly — the cask it's matured in. Expect more emphasis on notes like malt, honey, orchard fruit, vanilla and spice from the wood, rather than smoke, medicinal or bonfire notes.

## Is one "better" than the other?

No — it's purely a style preference. Non-peated whisky tends to be a friendlier entry point for people new to whisky, since smoke can be a polarising flavour, while peated whisky has a devoted following precisely because of that same intensity. Most New Zealand whisky is non-peated by default, simply because peat isn't a traditional local raw material, though some producers do release peated or lightly peated expressions using imported peated malt.
