The glass you use can genuinely change how much you get out of a whisky — mostly because of how it directs aroma toward your nose.

## The tasting glass: Glencairn-style / tulip glass

A narrow, tulip-shaped glass that curves inward toward the rim (the Glencairn glass is the best-known example) concentrates aromas and funnels them toward your nose as you bring the glass up. This shape is now the standard for professional whisky tasting and judging, and it's the best choice if you're seriously trying to evaluate a whisky's nose and flavour.

## The classic tumbler ("rocks glass")

A short, heavy-based tumbler is the traditional choice for a casual whisky serve, especially over ice or with a splash of water/mixer. It's less effective for concentrating aroma than a tulip glass, but it's practical, robust, and well suited to a relaxed drink rather than a formal tasting.

## The copita / sherry-style glass

Similar in principle to a Glencairn but often used by professional blenders and distillers for critical evaluation — narrow-bowled with a stem, designed to concentrate aroma even more precisely.

## The bottom line

For serious tasting and comparing whiskies side by side, a tulip-shaped glass is worth the small investment. For everyday casual drinking — especially over ice — a standard tumbler works perfectly well.
