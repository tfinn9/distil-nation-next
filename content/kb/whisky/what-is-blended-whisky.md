Blended whisky is created by combining different whiskies — typically **malt whisky** (rich in character, made from malted barley in pot stills) and **grain whisky** (lighter, smoother, made from other grains in column stills) — to hit a specific, repeatable flavour profile.

## Why blend at all?

A master blender's job is consistency: a big blended whisky brand needs to taste essentially the same bottle after bottle, year after year, even though individual casks vary naturally. Blending lets a producer smooth out that cask-to-cask variation and combine the character of malt whisky with the lighter, more mixable body of grain whisky.

## Blended whisky vs single malt

- **Single malt** — one distillery, 100% malted barley — see our dedicated article for the full breakdown.
- **Blended whisky** — a mix of malt and grain whisky, often sourced from several distilleries, assembled by a blending house rather than distilled entirely on one site.

## Is blended whisky "lower quality"?

Not inherently — blending is a skill in its own right, and some of the world's best-selling and most technically impressive whiskies are blends. Blended whisky tends to be more affordable and more mixer-friendly than single malt, which is part of why it dominates in cocktails and highball-style serves, but that's a difference in style and price positioning, not a straightforward quality hierarchy.
