There's no single "correct" way to taste whisky, but a simple, repeatable process helps you notice far more than just sipping straight away.

## 1. Look

Note the colour, though remember it can be influenced by cask type, age, and (in some markets) added caramel colouring — it's a weak indicator on its own.

## 2. Nose it gently

Bring the glass to your nose and inhale gently rather than deeply — whisky's high ABV can numb your sense of smell if you inhale too hard, a sensation often called "alcohol burn" on the nose. Try nosing with your mouth slightly open, and from a little distance before going in close.

## 3. Let it breathe

Give the glass a minute or two after pouring, and after your first nosing pass. Aromas open up and change as the spirit sits and as some of the alcohol vapour dissipates.

## 4. Take a small sip and let it sit

Rather than swallowing immediately, let the whisky sit on your tongue for a few seconds. Notice how the flavour develops and changes before you swallow.

## 5. Note the finish

After swallowing, pay attention to what lingers — how long the flavour lasts, and whether it changes character as it fades (a short vs long finish, and whether it stays pleasant or turns bitter/hot).

## 6. Try it again with a drop of water

A small amount of water can change how a whisky presents, sometimes opening up aromas that alcohol was masking — see "Should you add water to whisky?" for more on when and why to do this.

## Use the right glass

A tulip-shaped glass (like a Glencairn) concentrates aromas toward your nose far better than a standard tumbler — see "What glass should you use for whisky?" for more detail.
