Whisky is far more stable than wine, but a few simple habits keep it tasting its best for longer.

## Store bottles upright

Unlike wine, whisky should be stored **standing up**, not on its side. Whisky's higher ABV can degrade a cork over long periods of constant contact, potentially affecting the seal and letting in unwanted air or, in rare cases, tainting the spirit with cork character.

## Keep it out of direct sunlight

UV light can gradually degrade some of the compounds in whisky and affect colour and flavour over long periods. A cupboard, cabinet or shelf out of direct sun is ideal.

## Avoid heat and big temperature swings

A stable, moderate room temperature is best. Avoid storing whisky somewhere that gets hot (like near an oven, a sunny windowsill, or an uninsulated garage in summer) or fluctuates significantly, as this can accelerate evaporation and subtly change the spirit over time.

## Once opened, don't leave it too long

An opened bottle has more airspace and more oxygen exposure than a sealed one. Whisky doesn't spoil the way food does, but a bottle that's been open for several years, especially if mostly empty, may taste slightly flatter or more oxidised than when first opened. As a rule of thumb, aim to finish an opened bottle within a year or two for the fullest flavour, though it will still be safe to drink well beyond that.

## No need to refrigerate

Unlike gin, whisky doesn't benefit from being stored in the freezer or fridge — room temperature is fine for both storage and serving (see "Should whisky be served neat, with water or on ice?" for serving temperature specifically).
