*This is a general planning framework. Specific opening days, prices and booking requirements vary by distillery and should always be confirmed directly before visiting.*

## What a typical distillery visit looks like

Distillery experiences in New Zealand's craft scene range widely, from a simple cellar-door tasting counter to a guided tour through the production space followed by a structured tasting flight. Smaller producers often run more informal, personal visits (sometimes with the distiller themselves on-site), while larger operations may offer scheduled tour times and a dedicated visitor centre.

## Tours vs cellar doors — know the difference

- A **cellar door** generally means you can drop in (sometimes by appointment) to taste and buy products, without necessarily seeing the production area.
- A **tour** typically means a guided walkthrough of the actual distilling process — the still, fermentation, and (for whisky/rum) the barrel room — usually followed by a tasting.

Based on our directory, tours are less common than cellar-door-only visits (20 distilleries list tours vs 39 with some form of cellar door/visitor centre), so if a guided tour specifically is what you want, check ahead rather than assuming it's available everywhere.

## Planning tips

- **Book ahead where possible** — many craft distilleries are small operations, sometimes staffed by the same person running production, so drop-in access isn't guaranteed.
- **Check opening days** — smaller producers often only open their cellar door on specific days or by appointment, rather than daily.
- **Ask about transport if drinking** — especially for regions with rural or spread-out distilleries, plan a designated driver or transport option in advance.
- **Combine with other local craft producers** — many distilleries sit near breweries, wineries or coffee roasters, making it easy to build a broader craft beverage day trip.
