New Zealand's craft distilling industry is built around small, independent producers, and that's exactly what makes visiting them so memorable.

Unlike many large international spirits brands, New Zealand distilleries are often family-owned businesses producing relatively small batches. In some cases, the person pouring your tasting flight might also be the person who designed the recipe, mashed the grain, ran the still, and bottled the spirit.

That creates a very different experience from a typical commercial tour.

Whether you're planning a dedicated spirits road trip or simply looking for something different to do while travelling, here's what you can expect from a New Zealand distillery visit.

## What happens during a distillery tour?

Every distillery offers something slightly different, but most visits follow a similar pattern.

A typical experience might include:

* A short introduction to the distillery and its history
* A walkthrough of the production process
* An explanation of ingredients and local botanicals
* A look at the still and production equipment
* A visit to the maturation or barrel storage area (if applicable)
* A guided tasting of several spirits
* An opportunity to purchase bottles directly from the producer

Tours can last anywhere from 30 minutes to several hours, depending on the distillery and the type of experience being offered.

## Distillery tours and cellar doors aren't the same thing

One of the most common misconceptions is that every distillery offering tastings also offers tours.

That's not always the case.

### Cellar doors

A cellar door is primarily a tasting and retail experience.

You can usually:

* Taste spirits
* Purchase bottles
* Ask questions about the products
* Learn more about the distillery

However, you may not have access to the production area.

### Distillery tours

A tour typically includes access to parts of the working distillery.

Depending on the producer, you might see:

* The still
* Fermentation tanks
* Botanical preparation areas
* Barrel rooms
* Bottling equipment

Based on the Distil-Nation NZ directory, cellar doors are significantly more common than formal guided tours.

If a behind-the-scenes experience is important to you, always check in advance rather than assuming tours are available.

## What types of spirits can you expect to taste?

That depends entirely on the distillery.

Many producers focus on a single category, while others produce multiple styles.

You might encounter:

* Botanical gin
* Single malt whisky
* Rum
* Vodka
* Moonshine
* Fruit liqueurs
* Barrel-aged spirits
* Seasonal or limited releases

One of the advantages of visiting smaller producers is that you're often able to taste experimental spirits that aren't widely available through retailers.

## Tips for planning your visit

### Book ahead

Many New Zealand distilleries are small operations.

The same person responsible for distilling may also be managing tastings, bottling, packaging, deliveries, and administration.

Booking ahead is almost always the safest option.

### Check opening hours carefully

Unlike wineries, many distilleries don't operate seven days a week.

Some are open only on weekends, while others require appointments for tastings or tours.

Always confirm opening hours directly with the distillery before travelling.

### Plan your transport

This should go without saying, but it's particularly important when visiting distilleries in rural locations.

If you plan to participate in tastings:

* Choose a designated driver
* Consider a guided tour operator
* Use local transport where possible
* Limit the number of distilleries you visit in a single day

### Build a craft beverage trail

Many distilleries are located near other artisan producers.

Consider combining your visit with:

* Breweries
* Wineries
* Coffee roasters
* Cheesemakers
* Farmers' markets
* Restaurants featuring local produce

Some of New Zealand's best food and beverage experiences happen when you combine several producers into a single day trip.

## Which regions are best for distillery tourism?

Several regions have developed particularly strong craft spirits communities, including:

* Auckland
* Canterbury
* Otago
* Wellington
* Waikato
* Bay of Plenty

Each region offers a different experience, from urban tasting rooms to rural distilleries surrounded by vineyards, farmland, mountains, or coastal landscapes.
