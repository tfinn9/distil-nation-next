*This is a general overview, not a compliance checklist. New Zealand alcohol labelling is governed by the Australia New Zealand Food Standards Code, which is periodically updated — verify current requirements directly against the Code (and with a compliance specialist) before relying on this for actual product labelling.*

New Zealand shares its food and alcohol labelling framework with Australia through the joint Food Standards Code, and packaged alcoholic beverages sold here are generally expected to display several categories of information.

## Alcohol content

**ABV (Alcohol By Volume)** must be shown on the label — see our article on what ABV means.

## Standard drinks

Packaging is generally required to show the **number of standard drinks** contained in the container, calculated from the volume and ABV — see "What is a standard drink in New Zealand?" for how that figure is worked out.

## Pregnancy warning

A **pregnancy health warning label** — advising against alcohol consumption during pregnancy — became a mandatory requirement for alcoholic beverages sold in New Zealand and Australia. The exact current wording/format requirements should be checked against the current Food Standards Code rather than assumed, since specific label design rules can be updated.

## Allergen and ingredient information

Depending on the product, allergen statements (for example, relevant to certain grain-based or dairy-based spirits) may be required under general food labelling rules that extend to alcohol.

## Producer/importer and origin information

Labels are generally expected to identify the producer or importer and include relevant contact/origin information, consistent with general consumer protection and food labelling requirements.

## Why this needs a proper compliance check

Food and alcohol labelling rules are detailed, periodically revised, and carry real regulatory consequences for getting wrong — a new distillery should work directly from the current Food Standards Code (and ideally a compliance specialist) rather than a general summary like this one when finalising real product labels.
