**Starting a distillery in New Zealand involves much more than buying a still and creating a recipe. Before you sell your first bottle, you'll need to understand the licences, registrations, approvals, and compliance requirements that may apply to your business.**

One of the biggest surprises for new distillers is that there isn't a single document called a "distillery licence" that gives you permission to do everything.

Instead, you'll usually be dealing with several different agencies, each responsible for a different part of the process.

*This article provides general information only and shouldn't be considered legal, tax, or business advice. Requirements vary depending on your location, business structure, and production model. Always confirm current requirements with the relevant authorities.*

## Registering your business

Before anything else, you'll need to decide how your business will operate.

Common business structures include:

* Sole trader
* Partnership
* Limited liability company

You'll also need to consider:

* Business registration
* GST registration
* Business banking
* Accounting systems
* Insurance

Many distilleries choose to establish a limited company before beginning commercial production.

## Customs and excise requirements

Because spirits are subject to excise duty, New Zealand Customs plays a significant role in the spirits industry.

Commercial producers may need to consider:

* Excise registration
* Excise accounting
* Record-keeping requirements
* Storage and manufacturing obligations
* Filing and payment requirements

Understanding excise should be one of your first priorities because it will have a direct impact on your cash flow and pricing.

## Food safety requirements

Alcohol is a food product.

That means food safety requirements may apply to your operation.

Depending on your products, you may need to consider:

* Food control plans
* Risk management programmes
* Manufacturing standards
* Product traceability
* Ingredient management

Products containing cream, dairy, fruit, honey, or other ingredients may have additional considerations beyond those of a traditional distilled spirit.

## Local council approvals

Before signing a lease or installing equipment, it's worth speaking with your local council.

Depending on your premises, you may need approvals relating to:

* Zoning
* Building consent
* Fire safety
* Waste disposal
* Trade waste
* Ventilation
* Hazardous substances

A warehouse that seems perfect for a distillery may not actually be approved for that type of manufacturing activity.

## Alcohol licensing

If you plan to sell alcohol directly to customers, you'll need to understand New Zealand's alcohol licensing system.

Depending on your business model, this might include licences relating to:

* Cellar doors
* Tastings
* On-site sales
* Off-site sales
* Events

The requirements for a production facility can be very different from those for a public tasting room.

## Product labelling requirements

Before a bottle reaches a customer, it also needs to comply with labelling requirements.

You'll need to think about things such as:

* Alcohol content declarations
* Bottle volume
* Product identification
* Allergen information
* Batch tracking

Labelling is often left until the end of the process, but it's much easier to build compliance into your packaging from the beginning.

## Insurance is just as important as licensing

It's easy to focus on permits and overlook insurance.

Many distilleries carry some combination of:

* Public liability insurance
* Product liability insurance
* Business insurance
* Equipment insurance
* Stock insurance

Distilleries work with flammable liquids, expensive equipment, and regulated products, so risk management shouldn't be an afterthought.

## Don't underestimate the cost of compliance

Many first-time distillers budget for:

* The still
* Bottles
* Ingredients
* Packaging

They often underestimate the cost of:

* Excise
* Compliance
* Insurance
* Professional advice
* Council approvals
* Laboratory testing
* Label development

Compliance isn't an optional extra. It's part of the cost of doing business.

## The bottom line

Starting a distillery in New Zealand doesn't usually involve obtaining a single licence.

Instead, it means navigating a network of registrations, approvals, tax obligations, food safety requirements, and local regulations.

Before investing in equipment, speak with the relevant authorities, develop a realistic budget, and build compliance into your business plan from day one.

It will almost certainly save you time, money, and frustration later.
