Starting a distillery in New Zealand generally involves several separate approval processes, not one single "distillery licence."

## 1. Excise manufacturer's licence (Customs NZ)

Because distilling spirits triggers excise duty obligations, you'll need to be licensed as an alcohol manufacturer with Customs NZ before you can legally produce spirits, even before you sell anything. This licensing is separate from — and a prerequisite to — actually selling your product.

## 2. Alcohol licensing (Sale and Supply of Alcohol Act 2012)

If you plan to sell alcohol directly — a cellar door, on-site tastings, or retail sales — you'll typically need to go through your local council's alcohol licensing process under the Sale and Supply of Alcohol Act 2012, which covers where and how alcohol can be sold and consumed.

## 3. Food safety / business registration

Producing a consumable product generally brings standard food safety compliance obligations, alongside the usual business registration, GST and other requirements that apply to any commercial venture in New Zealand.

## 4. Resource consent / building and zoning considerations

Depending on your site and the scale of your operation, local council resource consent, building consent or zoning rules may also apply — particularly if you're setting up in a building not previously used for manufacturing, or if you're planning visitor-facing facilities like a cellar door or tasting room.

## Where to start

Given how many separate bodies are involved (Customs NZ, your local council, and potentially others), the practical first step for anyone serious about opening a distillery is to contact Customs NZ directly about excise licensing, and your council about alcohol licensing and consents, rather than relying on a general guide like this one for exact requirements.
