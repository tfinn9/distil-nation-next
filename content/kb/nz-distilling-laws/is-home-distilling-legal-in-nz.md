**Yes. Home distilling is legal in New Zealand for personal use.**

That answer surprises many people because New Zealand is one of the few countries in the world that allows adults to legally distil spirits at home.

In many countries, producing your own whisky, rum, gin, or vodka requires a licence or is prohibited altogether. In New Zealand, the law takes a different approach, allowing individuals to distil spirits for their own consumption.

However, there's an important distinction:

**Making spirits for yourself is legal. Selling them is not.**

*This article provides general information only and shouldn't be considered legal advice. Always confirm current legal requirements with the relevant authorities.*

## What does home distilling include?

Home distilling generally refers to producing spirits on a small scale for personal consumption.

This might include:

* Distilling whisky
* Making gin using a neutral spirit base
* Producing rum
* Experimenting with fruit spirits
* Creating botanical spirits
* Learning distillation as a hobby

Many hobbyists begin with small tabletop stills before moving on to larger equipment as they gain experience.

## Why do so many people think it's illegal?

Because in most countries, it is.

The United States, the United Kingdom, Australia, and Canada all heavily regulate the production of distilled spirits.

Beer and wine are often treated differently, with home brewing permitted while spirit production remains restricted.

As a result, many people assume the same rules apply in New Zealand.

They don't.

## Can you sell spirits that you make at home?

No.

Once you begin producing spirits for commercial purposes, an entirely different set of rules applies.

Commercial distilleries may need to comply with requirements relating to:

* Excise duty
* Customs registration
* Food safety
* Product labelling
* Alcohol licensing
* Manufacturing regulations

If you're considering selling spirits, it's important to understand those requirements before investing in equipment or developing products.

## Is there a limit to how much you can distil?

There's no simple volume limit that separates legal from illegal production.

The key distinction is whether the spirits are genuinely being produced for personal use or whether they're being produced as part of a commercial activity.

If you're producing large quantities or regularly supplying other people, you should seek advice before assuming your activity still falls within the definition of personal use.

## Is home distilling safe?

Just because something is legal doesn't mean it's risk-free.

Distillation involves:

* Flammable alcohol vapours
* High temperatures
* Heated equipment
* Pressurised systems

Poorly designed equipment or unsafe operating practices can create serious hazards.

If you're new to distilling, take the time to understand the process before operating a still.

## Common myths about home distilling

### Myth: Home distilling is illegal in New Zealand.

**False.** Adults can legally distil spirits for personal use.

### Myth: Homemade spirits contain methanol.

**Misleading.** Methanol is naturally produced during fermentation, but modern distillation practices and properly designed equipment help manage it. Following established distilling practices is essential.

### Myth: You can sell homemade spirits if you're only making small batches.

**False.** Selling spirits introduces commercial and regulatory obligations regardless of batch size.

## The bottom line

New Zealand has one of the world's most permissive approaches to home distilling.

If you're producing spirits for your own consumption, home distilling is legal.

If you're producing spirits to sell, you'll need to understand the regulations that apply to commercial production.
