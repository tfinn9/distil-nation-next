Yes, importing spirits into New Zealand is legal, whether for personal use (within set limits) or commercial resale, but it comes with several layers of compliance.

## Duty and tax on imported spirits

Imported spirits generally attract an excise-equivalent duty (calculated in a similar way to the excise duty paid on locally made spirits — see "How does excise duty work in New Zealand?"), plus GST on the total landed value including duty. The exact current rates need to be checked directly with Customs NZ, since — like local excise — they're periodically adjusted.

## Personal allowances vs commercial importing

Travellers bringing alcohol into New Zealand for personal use benefit from duty-free personal allowances up to a certain quantity, beyond which duty applies. Commercial importing for resale is a different process entirely, generally requiring proper import documentation and, depending on volume, potentially its own compliance obligations similar to those covering local manufacturers. Exact current allowance thresholds should be confirmed directly with Customs NZ before travelling or planning a commercial import.

## Biosecurity considerations

The Ministry for Primary Industries (MPI) has biosecurity requirements that can apply to alcohol imports, particularly around packaging materials and any agricultural elements (like herbs or botanicals) that might accompany a product — this is worth checking for any importer bringing in less conventional or artisanal products.

## Labelling compliance still applies

Imported spirits sold in New Zealand still need to meet local labelling requirements (standard drinks, allergen information, etc.) — see "What are New Zealand's alcohol labeling requirements?"
