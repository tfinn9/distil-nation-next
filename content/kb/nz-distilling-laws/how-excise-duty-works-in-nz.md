## The basic mechanism

New Zealand charges excise duty on alcohol based on the **litres of pure alcohol (LPA)** contained in a product, rather than a flat rate per bottle or per litre of liquid. This means a higher-ABV spirit is taxed more heavily, per litre of liquid, than a lower-ABV drink like beer or wine — which is a big part of why spirits are generally more expensive per standard drink than beer.

## Who pays it, and when

Excise is generally paid by the licensed manufacturer (the distillery) or importer, calculated on the alcohol content of what's produced or brought into the country, and administered by Customs NZ (which, despite the name, also handles domestic excise on alcohol manufacturing, not just imports).

## Rates change over time

Excise rates on alcohol in New Zealand are periodically adjusted — historically, alcohol excise rates have been reviewed and increased in line with inflation on a recurring schedule set by government. Because these rates change, **any specific dollar figure quoted here would likely go stale quickly** — we'd need to pull the current rate directly from Customs NZ at the time of publishing rather than hard-code a number that could mislead readers.

## What this means for distilleries

Excise is a major cost input for any spirits producer, and it's charged before the product ever reaches a shelf — meaning it's baked into pricing from the start, not added on top at the point of sale the way GST is.
