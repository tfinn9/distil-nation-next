**Excise duty is a tax charged on alcohol produced in or imported into New Zealand. For spirits producers, it's one of the biggest costs of doing business.**

If you've ever wondered why a bottle of craft gin costs more than you expected, excise duty is a big part of the answer.

Unlike GST, which applies to most goods and services, excise duty is a specific tax calculated on the amount of alcohol in a product.

The stronger the spirit, the more excise you'll generally pay.

*This article provides general information only and shouldn't be considered tax, customs, or legal advice. Excise rates change over time, so always confirm current rates and requirements with the relevant authorities.*

## What is excise duty?

Excise duty is a government tax applied to certain products, including:

* Alcohol
* Tobacco
* Some fuel products

For alcoholic beverages, the tax is usually based on the amount of pure alcohol contained in the product rather than the retail price.

That means two bottles of the same size can attract very different amounts of excise depending on their alcohol content.

## Who pays excise duty?

Commercial producers and importers are generally responsible for paying excise duty.

That includes businesses that:

* Distil spirits
* Manufacture alcoholic products
* Import alcohol into New Zealand

Consumers don't usually pay excise directly.

However, the cost of excise is typically built into the final retail price.

## How is excise on spirits calculated?

Spirits are usually taxed according to:

* Alcohol by volume (ABV)
* The volume of the product
* The current excise rate

The calculation is based on the amount of pure alcohol in the bottle.

For example:

A 700ml bottle at 40% ABV contains 280ml of pure alcohol.

A 700ml bottle at 57% ABV contains 399ml of pure alcohol.

Because the second bottle contains more alcohol, it will generally attract more excise duty.

## Why does excise have such a big impact on craft distilleries?

Large producers can spread their costs across huge production volumes.

Small craft distilleries can't.

Excise can represent a significant portion of the final retail price of a bottle, alongside other costs such as:

* Ingredients
* Packaging
* Bottles and closures
* Labels
* Labour
* Rent
* Insurance
* Distribution
* Marketing

This is one of the reasons craft spirits often cost more than consumers expect.

## Does home distilling attract excise duty?

Home distilling for personal use is legal in New Zealand.

Commercial production is different.

Once spirits are being produced for sale, excise obligations may apply.

If you're moving from hobby distilling into commercial production, understanding excise should be one of your first priorities.

## Do imported spirits also attract taxes?

Yes.

Imported spirits may be subject to:

* Excise-equivalent duties
* GST
* Import-related charges

The amount payable will depend on the product and the circumstances of the import.

## How often do excise rates change?

Excise rates aren't fixed forever.

Rates can be reviewed and adjusted, which means the amount payable today may not be the same in the future.

For that reason, it's always worth checking the latest published rates rather than relying on older calculations.

## Where can you find current excise rates?

The most up-to-date information is available directly from New Zealand Customs.

If you're starting a distillery or importing spirits commercially, official sources should always be your primary reference.

## The bottom line

Excise duty is one of the most important taxes affecting New Zealand's spirits industry.

The more alcohol a product contains, the more excise will generally be payable.

For consumers, excise helps explain why premium spirits can be expensive.

For distilleries, it's a major business cost that affects pricing, profitability, and long-term sustainability.
