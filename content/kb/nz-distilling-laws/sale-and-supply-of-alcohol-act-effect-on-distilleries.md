It's worth separating two different legal questions a distillery faces: **"Am I allowed to make this spirit?"** (governed by excise licensing through Customs NZ) and **"Am I allowed to sell it directly to customers on-site or elsewhere?"** (governed by the Sale and Supply of Alcohol Act 2012). The Sale and Supply of Alcohol Act is the one relevant to cellar doors, tasting rooms, and direct retail sale.

## What the Act generally covers

The Sale and Supply of Alcohol Act 2012 sets out the licensing framework for selling and supplying alcohol in New Zealand generally — covering things like on-licences (for on-site consumption, such as a cellar door or bar), off-licences (for takeaway/retail sale), and the conditions and hours under which alcohol can be sold.

## Why this matters for a distillery specifically

A distillery that wants to run a cellar door, offer tastings, or sell bottles directly to visitors will typically need to go through the same local alcohol licensing process that any bar, bottle store or restaurant would, administered through the relevant district licensing committee at the local council — this is separate from, and in addition to, the excise manufacturer's licence needed to produce the spirit in the first place.

## Practical implication for planning a distillery

Because this licensing runs through local councils rather than a single national body, requirements and processing times can vary by region — worth factoring into any timeline for opening a distillery with a customer-facing tasting or retail component.
