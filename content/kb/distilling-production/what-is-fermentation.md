Fermentation is the biological engine behind every spirit — distillation can only concentrate alcohol that fermentation has already created.

## The basic chemistry

Yeast consumes sugar and, through its metabolism, produces ethanol (alcohol) and carbon dioxide as by-products. Written simply:

```
sugar + yeast → alcohol + carbon dioxide (+ flavour compounds)
```

## What gets fermented

- **Grain-based spirits** (whisky, vodka) ferment the sugary wort produced by mashing (see "What is mashing?").
- **Rum** ferments molasses or cane juice directly, since those already contain fermentable sugar without needing a starch-conversion step.
- **Gin's base spirit** goes through the same grain fermentation process as vodka, before botanicals are ever introduced.

## How long it takes

Fermentation typically runs anywhere from a couple of days to a few weeks, depending on the yeast strain, temperature, and the flavour profile the distiller wants. Faster fermentations tend to be cleaner; slower fermentations often develop more complex, sometimes fruitier or funkier flavour compounds (see "How does yeast affect flavour?").

## What comes out the other end

The result is a low-alcohol liquid — often called a "wash" (for whisky/grain spirits) or simply fermented wort/juice — typically in the range of 6–10% ABV, similar in strength to a beer or cider. This wash is what then gets distilled to concentrate the alcohol and refine the flavour into the final spirit.
