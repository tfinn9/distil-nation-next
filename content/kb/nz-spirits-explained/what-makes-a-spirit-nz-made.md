There's no single, universal label that automatically makes a spirit "New Zealand made" — but in practice, most people (and most distillers) mean a combination of the following.

## Distilled in New Zealand

At minimum, "New Zealand made" almost always means the actual distillation happened here — the mash was fermented and run through a still on New Zealand soil, by a New Zealand-based producer, rather than the finished spirit simply being imported and bottled locally.

## Locally sourced ingredients (often, but not always)

Many New Zealand distillers go further and highlight local sourcing: New Zealand grain, New Zealand water, and — especially for gin — native or locally foraged botanicals like mānuka, kawakawa, horopito or native citrus. This is a marketing and provenance choice rather than a strict legal requirement, so it's worth checking a specific brand's own claims rather than assuming every "NZ made" spirit uses 100% local raw materials. Some ingredients (molasses for rum, agave for spirits in that style, certain grains) may still be imported even by an otherwise NZ-based distillery, simply because New Zealand doesn't grow them.

## Bottled and finished here

Most New Zealand distilleries also bottle on-site or through a local bottling partner, which supports the "made here" claim end-to-end rather than just at the distillation stage.

## A general-purpose "Buy New Zealand Made" claim

There is a well-known "Buy New Zealand Made" campaign/logo used across many product categories in New Zealand, with its own criteria for what qualifies. If you want to reference that specific certification and its exact rules for spirits, that should be checked directly against the campaign's current criteria rather than assumed — it's a separate scheme from simply being distilled in NZ.
