Craft distilling in New Zealand isn't confined to one region — but some areas have a much denser concentration of producers than others. Based on the Distil-Nation NZ directory, here's the current regional spread (by number of listed distilleries):

- **Auckland** — 29 (the largest concentration by far)
- **Canterbury** — 14
- **Otago** — 12
- **Waikato** — 9
- **Bay of Plenty** — 8
- **Wellington** — 7
- **Marlborough** — 7
- **Tasman** — 4
- **Taranaki** — 4
- **Southland** — 4
- **Manawatū-Whanganui** — 4
- **Hawke's Bay** — 3
- **West Coast** — 1
- **Northland** — 1

## Why Auckland dominates

As New Zealand's largest city, Auckland has the biggest concentration of population, hospitality venues and disposable income to support craft spirit brands — plus easier access to imported ingredients, equipment and distribution. It's a similar dynamic to why Auckland also leads in craft beer and specialty coffee.

## Notable regional specialities

- **Otago (especially Central Otago and Waitaki)** has become closely associated with New Zealand whisky, thanks to producers like Cardrona Distillery and the historic Willowbank-lineage whisky makers around Ōamaru.
- **Canterbury** has a strong cluster of gin and whisky producers, including Christchurch-based operations.
- Gin production is spread fairly evenly nationwide — it doesn't require the long lead times of whisky, so it's a common starting spirit for new distilleries in almost every region.
