There's no single "correct" way to drink whisky — the right choice depends on the specific whisky, and what you're in the mood for.

## Neat

Serving whisky neat (no ice, no water) shows it exactly as it was bottled — full strength, full intensity. This is the best way to properly evaluate a whisky's character, especially for tasting and comparing bottles, though higher-ABV or cask-strength whiskies can taste hot and closed-off neat for some drinkers.

## With a splash of water

A small amount of water can lower the ABV slightly, which often opens up aromatics that alcohol strength was suppressing — see "Should you add water to whisky?" for more detail. This is a common approach used by professional tasters and blenders, not just a beginner's crutch.

## On ice ("on the rocks")

Ice chills the whisky and dilutes it gradually as it melts, which mellows the intensity and can make a bolder or higher-proof whisky more approachable, especially in warm weather or for a longer, more relaxed drink. The trade-off is that as the ice melts, the drink becomes progressively more diluted, so the flavour balance shifts over the course of the drink — some whisky enthusiasts prefer to avoid this for delicate or expensive bottles.

## The practical answer

Try a whisky neat first if you're getting to know it, then experiment with a few drops of water or ice to see how it changes. For everyday, easy-drinking pours — especially in a mixed setting or over a longer session — ice or water is a completely valid and common way to enjoy whisky, not a lesser one.
