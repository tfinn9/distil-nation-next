You can make excellent cocktails at home with a surprisingly small toolkit — quality and technique matter more than having every gadget.

## The essentials

- **Cocktail shaker** — a Boston shaker (two-piece, glass-and-metal) or a cobbler shaker (three-piece, with a built-in strainer) both work well; pick whichever you find easier to use.
- **Jigger (measure)** — precise measuring is the single biggest factor in making a balanced cocktail consistently. A double-sided jigger (commonly 30ml/60ml or similar) covers most standard recipes.
- **Bar spoon** — a long-handled spoon for stirring drinks (like a Martini or Negroni) and layering ingredients.
- **Strainer** — a Hawthorne strainer (with a coiled spring) fits over a shaker to strain out ice and any solids when pouring.
- **Fine strainer (optional but useful)** — a small mesh strainer used alongside a Hawthorne strainer for "double straining," which removes small ice shards or citrus pulp for a cleaner-looking drink.

## Nice-to-haves

- **A sharp paring knife and cutting board** — for citrus garnishes and fresh ingredients.
- **A citrus juicer/press** — fresh juice makes a genuinely noticeable difference over bottled juice in almost every classic recipe.
- **A muddler** — for cocktails like a Mojito or Old Fashioned with fruit, where you need to gently mash ingredients to release flavour.
- **Good ice trays or moulds** — see "What ice should you use for cocktails?" for why this matters more than it seems.

## What you can skip starting out

Specialty tools — channel knives, atomiser bottles, elaborate garnish kits — are fun once you're deeper into the hobby, but none of them are necessary to make a genuinely excellent Old Fashioned, Negroni or Daiquiri with the core list above.
