The Old Fashioned is one of the oldest cocktail formats still in common use, and its simplicity means every ingredient really matters.

## Classic recipe

- **60ml whisky** (bourbon or rye is traditional; a good New Zealand whisky works well too)
- **1 sugar cube or ~5ml simple syrup**
- **2 dashes aromatic bitters** (an Angostura-style bitters is the classic choice)
- **Orange peel**, for garnish and aromatic oils
- Ice (ideally one large cube)

## Method

1. Add the sugar cube (or syrup) and bitters to a mixing glass.
2. Add a splash of water and stir/muddle briefly to dissolve the sugar if using a cube.
3. Add the whisky and a large ice cube.
4. Stir gently for around 20–30 seconds to chill and slightly dilute.
5. Strain (or simply serve as-is if already built in the serving glass) over a fresh large ice cube in a rocks glass.
6. Express the oils from a piece of orange peel over the drink (twist it near the surface to release the aromatic oils), then either drop it in or use it purely as a garnish.

## Common mistakes

- **Over-diluting** — stirring too long, or using small ice that melts fast, waters the drink down too much.
- **Too much sugar** — an Old Fashioned should taste primarily of whisky, with sugar and bitters balancing rather than dominating.
- **Skipping the citrus oils** — the orange peel's aromatic oils are a key part of the drink's smell and first impression; don't skip expressing them over the glass.

## Whisky choice

A bolder, higher-proof whisky generally holds up better in an Old Fashioned than a very delicate or expensive single malt, since the sugar, bitters and dilution will all interact with (and soften) the spirit's character.
