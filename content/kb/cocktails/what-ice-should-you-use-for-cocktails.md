Ice isn't just a way to make a drink cold — it directly controls dilution, which is a core part of a cocktail's final balance.

## Why size and density matter

Smaller ice (like standard fridge-tray cubes or crushed ice) has more surface area relative to its volume, so it melts faster and dilutes a drink more quickly. Larger, denser ice (big cubes or spheres) melts more slowly, keeping a drink cold with less dilution over time.

## Shaking

For shaken cocktails (Daiquiris, Whisky Sours, Espresso Martinis), standard-sized cubes work well — the shaking process is quick, and you generally want some dilution as part of the recipe's balance.

## Stirring

For stirred, spirit-forward drinks (Martinis, Negronis, Old Fashioneds), larger cubes are preferable — they chill and dilute the drink more gently and evenly over a longer stir, without over-diluting.

## Serving "on the rocks"

A single large cube or a big ice sphere is ideal for a drink meant to be sipped slowly over ice (like a whisky on the rocks) — it keeps the drink cold for a long time while minimising how much it waters down before you finish it.

## Clarity matters too

Clear ice (made from boiled/filtered water, or using specialty directional-freezing methods) looks better and, because it contains fewer trapped air bubbles, can melt slightly more slowly than cloudy ice from a standard tray — mostly a presentation upgrade, though it does have a small practical benefit.

## The simple takeaway

Match your ice to your technique: smaller for quick shaking, bigger for slow stirring or sipping — and don't skimp on quantity when shaking, since under-filling a shaker with ice actually leads to faster, less even dilution.
